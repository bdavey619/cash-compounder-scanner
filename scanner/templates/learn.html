What is a company actually worth?

Four ideas, explained with a lemonade stand, are all you need to read this site. It takes about four minutes.

1A business is worth the cash it makes for its owners

Your lemonade stand sells $500 of lemonade a year. Lemons, sugar and cups cost $250. You pay a friend $150 to help.

What’s left, $100, is spare cash the owner could take home. Investors call this free cash flow. Real companies work exactly the same way, just with billions instead of hundreds.

The companies on this site keep at least 15¢ of every $1 of sales as spare cash, every single year. Your stand keeps 20¢, so it would make the list.

2Cash back per $1: is the price a good deal?

Say someone offers to sell you the whole stand. If you pay $1,000, it hands you $100 of spare cash a year: 10¢ back for every $1 you paid. That’s its cash yield.

Is 10¢ good? Compare it with the safest option: lending to the US government with a 10-year bond, which pays about a year with almost no risk. A business is riskier than a bond (lemons could get expensive, a rival could open next door), so it should pay you more.

3The price is a bet on the future

Why would anyone pay $3,000 for a stand that makes $100 a year? Only if they expect it to make a lot more later: a second stand, higher prices, more customers. So every price contains a hidden bet about how fast the cash will grow.

This site works that bet out backwards. We ask: how fast would the cash have to grow every year for the next 10 years to make today’s price fair? Then we compare that with how fast the company actually grew in the past.

When the price’s bet is far below the company’s past growth, the market expects things to get worse. Sometimes it’s right. Sometimes it’s too gloomy, and that’s where opportunities can hide.

What this site can’t tell you: whether a company’s problems are temporary or permanent. It shows you which businesses make lots of cash and what their prices are betting on. Deciding whether the bet is wrong is your job, and it’s a list for research, not a list of stocks to buy.
