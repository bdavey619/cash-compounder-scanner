Method

A great business is only a great investment at the right price. This finds the businesses that reliably turn revenue into cash, ranks them by how much cash you get per dollar, and shows what growth each price assumes. It is a starting point for research, not a list of things to buy.

What the backtest found

Replaying the rules at every month-end since 2014, using only what was knowable at the time: buying quality companies right after a sharp drop was a lottery ticket (a few big winners, a typical pick that lagged), and a sharp drop without a valuation gap was a reliable loser. Ranking by free-cash-flow yield worked when the 10-year Treasury yield was above 3% and failed when it was below, which is why the rate environment is shown on the home page. The high-rate months are mostly one stretch, 2022 onward, so treat that as a bet, not a law. See the full backtest

Blind spots

  • The screen can’t tell a temporary problem from a permanent one. That judgment is yours.
  • Companies whose filings don’t use standard tags for cash flow or share counts can be missing or, rarely, misread. Each company page links to its filings so you can check.
  • Free cash flow doesn’t mean the same thing for banks, lenders, insurers and real-estate companies, so they’re excluded.
  • The growth record looks back four fiscal years; a business that just changed shape (a spin-off, a big acquisition) can look odd.

Data: SEC EDGAR company facts (10-K and 10-Q), SEC submissions and Form 4 filings, daily closing prices, and the 10-year Treasury yield. Updated every weekday after the close. Source code: GitHub.
