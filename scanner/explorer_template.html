How the screen works
1. Quality filterFCF margin of 15%+, FCF ÷ capital of 10%+, positive FCF in each of the last 3 fiscal years and the trailing 12 months, $2B+ market cap. Removes stocks that are cheap for good reason.
2. Event triggerPrice down 10%+ more than the S&P 500 in 5 trading days, or 2+ insiders each buying $25k+ on the open market within 7 days (SEC Form 4).
3. Modest expectationsThe price implies 10%/yr or less, at least 5 points below reference growth (the lower of FCF and revenue growth, capped at 20%), at a 9% required return.
4. Your judgmentIs the problem temporary or permanent? The screen can't answer this. It only tells you where to look.

Read before relying on this

  • Volatile cash flow (orange badge) marks cyclical businesses such as memory chips or energy. Their price often assumes a slowdown because one usually follows a boom. Switch the cash-flow starting point to the 3-year average to judge them through a whole cycle.
  • Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital expenditures as reported in 10-K and 10-Q filings. Stock-based compensation is not subtracted.
  • Net cash is cash plus short-term investments minus long-term debt, from the latest filing.
  • The cash-flow starting-point switch lets you compare the trailing twelve months with the last full fiscal year.
